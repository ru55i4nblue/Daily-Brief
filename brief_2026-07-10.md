**Selected: Unilever weighs a bid for Thorne**

Unilever is exploring a bid for US supplements maker Thorne in a deal that could value the company at around $4 billion, according to Financial Times reporting from late June. Thorne, founded in 1984 and based in Summerville, South Carolina, makes clinical-grade supplements, powders and diagnostics, and passed $500 million in annual revenue in 2025 with a track toward $650 million in 2026.

Thorne's private-equity owner, L Catterton — which took the company private in a $680 million deal in 2023 — has launched a sale process that has drawn multiple bidders, reportedly including consumer-health group Haleon alongside Unilever. A Thorne deal would extend Unilever's recent run of wellness acquisitions: Liquid IV, Nutrafol, Olly, SmartyPants, and Grüns (bought in April). CEO Fernando Fernandez, in the role since March 2025, has been steering the group's portfolio toward beauty and wellness.

Why it matters: it's a clear signal that a major FMCG group sees personalised, clinical-leaning supplements as a growth category worth paying a premium for, as core packaged-goods growth stalls. A deal at the reported price would value Thorne at roughly 6x forward revenue.

Counterpoints: that multiple assumes Thorne's growth rate holds, in a supplement category facing rising regulatory scrutiny; folding a premium, clinically-positioned brand into a mass-market FMCG portfolio risks diluting the credibility that makes it valuable; and this is only an "exploration" — a competing Haleon bid could price Unilever out entirely.

Conversation starters:
- Does putting a premium clinical-supplements brand inside a mass-market FMCG group risk diluting what makes it valuable?
- With Liquid IV, Nutrafol, Olly, SmartyPants and possibly Thorne under one roof, is Unilever building a genuine wellness platform or just chasing a trend?
- If Haleon outbids Unilever, what does that say about how consumer-health giants value diagnostics-led wellness brands?
