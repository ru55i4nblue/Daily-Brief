**Selected: George Lucas's billion-dollar museum**

The Lucas Museum of Narrative Art opens to the public in Los Angeles on 22 September, capping a project George Lucas and his wife Mellody Hobson have pursued for over a decade after failed bids to site it in Chicago and San Francisco. The 300,000-square-foot building in Exposition Park, designed by Ma Yansong of MAD Architects with Stantec, holds more than 100,000 square feet of gallery space across 30-plus galleries, and its permanent collection spans over 40,000 works — from Norman Rockwell and Frida Kahlo to Frank Frazetta and Robert Crumb — alongside the complete Lucas Archives (1971–2012).

The founding premise is that illustration, comics, film and popular art belong in the same institutional category as fine art, told through a single lens: narrative. An opening night on 19 September, co-chaired by figures including Steven Spielberg, Guillermo del Toro and Pharrell Williams, signals the project's ambitions to be treated as a major cultural institution rather than a vanity showcase for one filmmaker's memorabilia.

Why it matters: museums dedicated to a single living billionaire's taste and collection are increasingly how major new cultural institutions get built in the US, given the scarcity of public funding for large-scale museum projects — this one reportedly cost well over $1 billion. It's a live test of whether "narrative art" holds together as a curatorial category, or ends up read as Lucas cross-legitimising Star Wars memorabilia via fine art.

Counterpoints: the collection's range (cave art to comics to cinema) is broad enough that critics may find it curatorially incoherent rather than boundary-expanding. It's also unclear whether "narrative art" survives as a framework once the initial press cycle fades, or whether visitors will simply treat it as a Star Wars museum with extra rooms.

Conversation starters:
- Does treating comics and film alongside fine art elevate popular culture, or dilute the category of "art"?
- Should billionaire-funded single-collector museums be judged by different standards than publicly funded institutions?
- Is "narrative" a genuine curatorial thread, or a framing device built to justify one collector's eclectic taste?
