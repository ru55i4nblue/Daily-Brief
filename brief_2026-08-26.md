**Selected: Soho House's identity crisis**

When Soho House's Miami Pool House opened in 2022, it was the brand distilled to its purest form — a pool, a cocktail list, a DJ booth. Last month it closed for a full refurbishment; when it reopens later this year it will be Soho Health Club, with an indoor-outdoor gym, contrast suites, a racquet club, hyperbaric oxygen, cryotherapy and a nutrient-focused restaurant — the company's largest South Florida investment since the club opened.

The renovation is the clearest signal yet of where the world's most recognisable members' club believes its future lies, and it's a long way from the hedonism the brand was built on. Health and fitness offerings now span 46 houses worldwide; longevity studios are planned for 180 House, Barcelona, New York and the forthcoming Los Cabos opening, and Soho Beach House is adding its own expanded spa and recovery wing.

It matters because Soho House's value has always rested on a specific cultural identity — a curated, faintly hedonistic social scene, not a fitness chain. A wholesale pivot toward longevity and wellness risks diluting that distinctiveness just as every competitor from boutique gyms to five-star hotels chases the same affluent, health-obsessed membership base, turning a differentiated brand into one more entrant in a crowded category.

**Counterpoints:** the wellness build-out may be additive rather than a replacement — bars, restaurants and DJ booths remain across most houses — and the shift could reflect a straightforward commercial calculation (higher-yield amenities, membership retention) rather than a deeper identity crisis.

**Conversation starters:**
- Is Soho House diversifying its offer, or diluting the identity that built it?
- Does the wellness pivot reflect genuine member demand, or an industry-wide trend every operator feels compelled to chase?
- If the "health club" model succeeds, does the original hedonistic Soho House format survive alongside it, or get quietly phased out?
