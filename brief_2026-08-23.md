**Selected: Emirates wants to make Milan a travel hub**

Emirates is close to winning approval for a Milan–Lima route that never touches Dubai, with Italian government sign-off possibly arriving as early as October and Los Angeles, São Paulo, Santiago and Mexico City reportedly next in line from Malpensa. These are fifth-freedom rights — permission for a foreign carrier to sell seats on a route between two other countries, provided the flight starts or ends at home. Emirates already runs Europe's best-known example, the Dubai–Milan–New York service it has flown on the A380 since 2013.

Lima sits beyond Dubai's nonstop range, which makes Milan the natural staging post, and northern Italy's wealthy catchment has gone underserved on long-haul since Alitalia's decline — a gap Lufthansa-owned ITA Airways hasn't closed. Every route Emirates adds from Malpensa is simultaneously a new market and a direct challenge on ITA's home turf. Emirates itself says it has no immediate plans to activate the flights, so the earliest realistic start is 2027.

**Why it matters:** fifth-freedom expansion is one of the sharper tools a Gulf carrier has for building scale without adding home-market capacity, and it exposes how thin European flag carriers' long-haul defences have become outside their core hubs. A win in Milan gives Emirates a template — and a threat — it can point at other underserved European cities.

**Counterpoints:** fifth-freedom rights depend entirely on political goodwill, and Emirates has been refused them elsewhere (notably a long-running US dispute over Gulf-carrier subsidies); Italy's approval isn't yet final. And "no immediate plans to activate" is doing a lot of work — this may be a rights land-grab for future leverage rather than a real 2027 route.

**Conversation starters:**
- Should national governments grant fifth-freedom rights to Gulf carriers if it undercuts their own flag carriers on long-haul routes?
- Is ITA Airways' passivity here a structural problem (thin long-haul fleet, Lufthansa's priorities) or a deliberate strategic retreat from unprofitable routes?
- If Emirates never activates the Lima flights, does securing the rights alone still change the competitive dynamic in Milan?
