**Selected: Audemars Piguet gets in on the pre-owned watch game**

Dedup: already expanded from this issue — Anthropic, low-cost flying, Apple MacBook Pro, Amex/Airbnb. The email carries headlines only; the expansion draws on trade-press coverage (WatchPro, Forbes, SJX), not the newsletter's own article.

Background: The secondary market for luxury watches has long been run by grey-market dealers and platforms, with brands earning nothing on resale and having no control over authenticity or pricing. Rolex moved first among the majors with a certified pre-owned programme sold through its retailers in late 2022.

Core finding: Audemars Piguet launched "The Watch Keepers" on 26 October 2026 in Switzerland and continental Europe. It is a consignment platform, not a dealership: AP neither buys nor sells. Sellers must have owned the watch for at least two years, ship it to an AP service centre for authentication and a stolen-watch check, and choose a fixed price or an auction. Each watch comes with a certificate and a two-year warranty. About 100 watches are listed at a time.

Why it matters: AP gains visibility over resale pricing and client data, discourages flipping through the two-year rule, and gives buyers a brand-backed alternative to dealers. It is a different model from Rolex's, which runs through retailers and restores watches.

Counterpoints: Watches are authenticated but not serviced or restored, so condition varies. Inventory of about 100 pieces is small against a market of many thousands of Royal Oaks, and the UK and US are excluded for now, so dealers are unlikely to be displaced soon. Fees and realised prices have not been confirmed here.

Conversation starters:

- Does a brand-run resale channel protect buyers, or mainly protect the brand's prices?
- Is a two-year ownership rule a fair way to curb flipping?
- Would you pay a premium for manufacturer authentication over a trusted independent dealer?
