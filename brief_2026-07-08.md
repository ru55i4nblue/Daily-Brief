**Selected: Luxottica heir wants control of the empire**

When Luxottica founder Leonardo Del Vecchio died in June 2022, ownership of Delfin S.à r.l. — the Luxembourg holding company controlling roughly 32% of EssilorLuxottica (Ray-Ban, Oakley, Sunglass Hut; $100B+ market cap) — split among his six children, his widow, and a stepson. Three years of infighting followed: frozen dividends, blocked governance decisions, paralysis at the top of one of Europe's largest corporate empires.

Leonardo Maria Del Vecchio (31, chairman of LMDV Capital) now wants to buy out the combined 25% Delfin stake held by siblings Luca and Paola for roughly €10 billion, part of a wider €40-56 billion Delfin restructuring. Completing it would push his stake to 37.5% — effectively resolving the succession standoff in his favour. He escalated by publishing an open letter, via a newspaper he owns (Quotidiano Nazionale), days before a 30 June shareholder meeting, arguing the dispute "stopped being financial and became a matter of governance" after the Delfin board reversed terms shareholders had already approved.

Why it matters: this settles control of Ray-Ban and Oakley's ultimate parent and sets a precedent for how founder-family successions play out at major European conglomerates.

Counterpoints: financing needs a ~€10B package from UniCredit, BNP Paribas and Crédit Agricole, and the banks are now demanding stronger dividend and capital-stability guarantees before committing. Luca and Paola have already asked a Luxembourg court to set a share price, which could force a transaction on terms outside Leonardo Maria's control. Delfin chairman Francesco Milleri is reportedly weighing a counter-proposal — having Delfin itself buy back the siblings' stakes and redistribute them among all six heirs — which would dilute Leonardo Maria's bid for outright control.
