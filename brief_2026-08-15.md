**Selected: The Rolex correction is complete**

Rolex's secondary market has been unwinding since its 2022 speculative peak, and by mid-2026 the correction looks structurally complete rather than still in freefall. WatchCharts' Rolex Price Index shows a 365-day change of roughly -22% as of 12 August 2026, with steel sports references — the Daytona, the Submariner — down 30–50% from their peak valuations even as Rolex keeps raising retail list prices.

The mechanism is what dealers call the "retail price trap": Rolex's boutique price increases pushed the gap between retail and grey-market ever higher during the boom, but as speculative buyers exited post-2022, resale premiums on steel sports models evaporated entirely for many references. The Daytona, which traded above $50,000 at the top, now sits in the mid-$30,000s. Precious-metal and discontinued references are holding up better, cushioned by genuine scarcity rather than hype.

It matters because it's a live case study in how a scarcity-manufactured luxury market behaves once speculative capital leaves: prices don't just soften, they can re-rate structurally while the brand's own pricing power keeps climbing in parallel. It also fits the pattern running through the rest of this week's Liber digest — wellness and luxury investment converging (Thorne's $3.8B sale, Equinox's expansion) even as older luxury benchmarks correct.

Counterpoints: a -22% index move looks large but follows a genuine speculative bubble, so it may simply be reversion to a still-elevated baseline rather than a "correction" in the pessimistic sense. And Rolex itself is insulated — it sells at fixed retail regardless of grey-market swings, so the correction is a secondary-market and reseller problem, not necessarily a brand-health one.

Conversation starters:
- Is the steel-sports correction a one-off unwind of 2021–22 speculation, or a preview of what happens to other hyped luxury categories?
- Does Rolex's insulation from resale prices (fixed retail, no official secondary market) make it structurally safer than brands more exposed to resale sentiment?
- With precious-metal and discontinued references holding value, is scarcity now a better collector strategy than brand name alone?
