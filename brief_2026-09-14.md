**Selected: David Protein and the $2.25 billion protein bet**

Medici Brands, maker of the David protein bar, raised $250 million this week at a $2.25 billion valuation — triple its worth a year ago — in a round led by Greenoaks and Valor Equity Partners with ICONIQ and Imaginary Ventures. David launched in September 2024 with a single product (28g protein, 150 calories, zero sugar) and is now in 35,000+ stores including Walmart, Target and Costco, with revenue heading past $300m a year.

Founder Peter Rahal previously built and sold RXBAR to Kellogg for $600m in 2017. This time the edge is Epogee, Medici's plant-based fat substitute that mimics fat's texture at a fraction of the calories — now being used to rebuild snack aisles via new brands HallPass (a low-sugar candy line) and Rowdy.

Why it matters: America's protein obsession has genuine upside (most people under-eat protein), and David proved a bar can taste decent at 150 calories. The valuation confirms how large the demand for "engineered" better-for-you snacking has become.

Counterpoints: the harder question is whether re-engineering junk food with lab-built ingredients and sweeteners is meaningfully different from the junk food it replaces — on that, the jury is still out. The bars remain a US-only proposition for now.

Conversation starters:
- Is "engineered" healthy food actually healthier, or just better marketed?
- Does Rahal's RXBAR-to-David repeat pattern suggest a repeatable playbook, or a hot moment in the category?
- What happens to David's growth once "AI-adjacent" protein and diet fads move on to the next thing?
