**Selected: Brunello Cucinelli is all in on AI**

Brunello Cucinelli has spent three years quietly building an AI company, and Salesforce has just taken a stake in it. Solomei AI, founded by the Italian designer in his home village in Umbria, recently replaced the brand's website with Callimacus, a platform built around AI agents that read a visitor's intent and assemble the shopping experience in real time rather than presenting pages, menus or categories — roughly what a good sales assistant does in store, done by software. The name honours Callimachus, the librarian of Alexandria, and the project grew from a collaboration between Cucinelli, Salesforce co-founder Marc Benioff, and a team the designer describes as including mathematicians, philosophers and humanists.

Salesforce's stake, of undisclosed size, will extend the platform to other retail clients across Europe and North America, making the self-styled "king of cashmere" the unlikely chairman of an AI company headquartered in a medieval hamlet. The deal still needs clearance under Italy's "golden power" rules governing strategic technology, so the paperwork is not yet finished.

**Why it matters:** it's a rare instance of a legacy luxury house building genuine AI infrastructure rather than licensing someone else's chatbot, and a bet that "pageless" commerce — software replicating human sales judgment rather than search-and-filter — is where premium retail is heading. A regulator's sign-off requirement also signals AI commerce platforms are starting to be treated as strategic technology, not just software.

**Counterpoints:** Callimacus is unproven at scale, and pageless browsing removes the comparison-shopping behaviour many customers actually want. It may also prove a luxury eccentricity — one designer's philosophical project — rather than a template other brands can replicate without Cucinelli's control over supply and brand voice.

**Conversation starters:**
- Does removing pages and menus improve luxury shopping, or does it just remove the customer's ability to browse and compare?
- Should national governments treat AI commerce platforms as strategic technology requiring regulatory clearance?
- Is Cucinelli's approach — philosophers on the founding team — a genuine model for "humane AI," or branding?
