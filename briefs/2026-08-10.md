**Selected: Thorne gets swallowed up**

Procter & Gamble announced on August 4, 2026 that it will acquire Thorne, the personalised-nutrition and supplements company, for $3.8 billion in cash from private equity owner L Catterton, with the deal expected to close in Q4 2026 pending regulatory approval. Founded in 1984, Thorne built its reputation on practitioner-trusted, science-backed supplements, at-home biomarker testing, and a subscription-driven direct-to-consumer model serving roughly 7 million customers. Its NSF Certified for Sport line — audited for more than 280 banned substances — makes it the trusted supplement partner for U.S. National Teams and a program recognised by USADA, MLB, the NHL, and the CFL, giving it credibility that's hard to replicate.

The price tag is striking: Thorne went public in 2021 at a $525 million valuation, was taken private by L Catterton in 2023 for $680 million, and has now roughly sextupled in under three years as revenue climbed past $500 million in 2025 toward a targeted $650 million in 2026 — putting the deal near 6x sales. CEO Shailesh Jejurikar framed the price as in line with Thorne's growth rate and said P&G is "really happy with the asset."

The deal matters because it signals P&G's push beyond household staples into faster-growing wellness categories to reach younger, health-conscious consumers, and it continues a wave of consumer-giant consolidation in supplements (Unilever bought Gruns in April). It also shows strategics valuing Thorne's testing-and-certification data loop, not just its products, as a durable moat.

Counterpoints: the roughly 6x-revenue price raises overpaying concerns, P&G already carries elevated debt that this deal adds to, and folding a nimble, practitioner-focused DTC brand into a massive CPG bureaucracy carries real integration and culture risk.

Conversation starters:
- Does P&G's wellness push signal where consumer-goods giants see future growth, or is this a sign the sector is running out of organic ideas?
- Can a scrappy, practitioner-trusted brand like Thorne keep its credibility once it's inside a corporate giant like P&G?
- With Unilever and P&G both buying supplement brands, is the wellness/supplements market headed for the same consolidation as big beverage or big food?
