**Selected: SpaceX IPO fuels uptick in private jet buyers**

SpaceX's long-rumoured IPO has minted a fresh cohort of newly liquid millionaires and billionaires among early employees and investors, and private aviation brokers are reporting a corresponding uptick in inquiries and sales from that group. The pattern echoes previous tech-liquidity events — Facebook's 2012 IPO and various crypto windfalls — where a sudden vesting of paper wealth into cash converts quickly into large discretionary purchases, and private jets are one of the fastest, most visible ways to deploy it. Brokers note interest skewing toward light and midsize jets and fractional-ownership programs rather than outright purchases of large-cabin aircraft, suggesting buyers are new to the category rather than seasoned owners trading up.

Why it matters: the private aviation market is a leading indicator of concentrated wealth creation events, and a SpaceX-driven bump signals just how much paper wealth the IPO unlocked in one stroke — likely tens of billions of dollars vesting to employees who were previously equity-rich but cash-poor. It also reflects broader normalisation of extreme wealth being spent quickly and visibly rather than reinvested.

Counterpoints: fractional and light-jet interest could just as easily reflect caution — new money hedging against buying more aircraft than it needs — rather than lavish spending. It's also not yet clear how much of the "uptick" is anecdotal broker chatter versus verified sales data, and IPO lock-up periods typically delay when employees can actually sell shares, meaning much of this liquidity may be anticipatory rather than realised.

Conversation starters:
1. Is a private jet purchase from IPO proceeds a rational diversification of sudden wealth, or a predictable pattern of newly-liquid money chasing status symbols?
2. How much of this "uptick" is real sales versus broker anecdote timed to the IPO news cycle?
