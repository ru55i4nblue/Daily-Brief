**Selected: American Express is cutting credit limits**

American Express is reportedly cutting spending limits for some of its heaviest personal-card spenders, with affected cardholders describing tens of thousands of dollars in spending power removed overnight and no advance warning. The accounts involved share a profile: high monthly spend, long account histories, and a record of on-time payments.

Amex's "no preset spending limit" cards have always worked dynamically, recalculated continuously against a range of factors, so the company is technically within its rights to cap spend at will. What's drawing attention is the pattern rather than any single case — limits reportedly cut for otherwise healthy customers all at once, over the past two weeks, which reads to some observers as a lender pulling back exposure across its book ahead of a downturn.

It matters because Amex's premium cardholder base is often treated as a bellwether for high-end consumer health, and unexplained, simultaneous limit cuts across "good" accounts are the kind of defensive signal lenders send when they expect credit conditions to worsen — regardless of what they say publicly.

**Counterpoints:** recession chatter comparing this to 2007 or 2019-style tightening comes from commentators and social media, not from Amex or independent data; and dynamic recalculation cutting some limits is business-as-usual for a "no preset limit" product, not necessarily evidence of a broader pullback. American Express has not commented on any policy change.

**Conversation starters:**
- Is a wave of unexplained limit cuts on "no preset limit" cards a meaningful leading indicator, or just algorithmic noise mistaken for a signal?
- Would Amex disclosing the actual trigger (spend category, utilisation, macro model) defuse the recession narrative, or confirm it?
- How should a cardholder react to a sudden limit cut with a clean payment history — is silence from the issuer itself the real red flag?
