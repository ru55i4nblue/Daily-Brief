**Selected: Neko Health adds body scans and wearables**

On 22 June 2026, Neko Health — the Stockholm-founded preventive-health startup co-founded by Spotify's Daniel Ek — announced the biggest upgrade to its full-body health scan since launch: body composition measurement and wearables integration. Every scan now includes visceral fat and body-fat percentage readings, captured by Neko's proprietary imaging device without X-ray radiation, giving clinicians a metabolic-health data point to work from during the consultation.

The wearables piece lets members connect the Neko app to Apple Health ahead of a booking, pulling in steps, sleep and heart-rate variability so that, on repeat visits, clinicians can explain year-on-year changes against lifestyle data rather than the scan number in isolation. Stockholm is also getting a next-generation scan rig with greater data volume and fidelity across skin, heart and circulation. The upgrade lands as Neko prepares a 2026 US expansion.

Why it matters: Neko's pitch is shifting healthcare from reactive treatment to continuous, data-driven monitoring — body composition and wearables integration push it further from a one-off "scan experience" toward an ongoing health record, which is the harder, stickier product to build and the one that justifies recurring visits.

Counterpoints: bundling wearables data into a clinical scan raises the stakes on data privacy and security for a company handling increasingly sensitive continuous health records; and more data points don't automatically mean better outcomes — the value depends entirely on whether Neko's clinicians can act on them, not just display them back to members.
