**Selected: Bentley's first EV, the Torcal**

Bentley has named its first fully electric vehicle the Torcal, with a reveal set for 23 September in London. The name comes from El Torcal de Antequera, a limestone rock formation in Andalusia, Spain — also a nod to the Latin *torquere* ("to twist"), referencing an electric motor's torque. Confirmed under 5m (197in) long, it sits below the Bentayga in size, with a touted range over 300 miles. It's expected to share its underpinnings with the upcoming Porsche Cayenne Electric on VW Group's 800-volt, dual-motor electric platform. The Torcal becomes Bentley's fourth model line alongside the Continental GT, Flying Spur and Bentayga — its only fully electric offering — and will be sold alongside the continuing petrol Bentayga rather than replacing it.

Why it matters: Bentley is hedging rather than committing outright to electrification, keeping a combustion flagship SUV alive well into the Torcal's life — a signal of real uncertainty about ultra-wealthy buyers' appetite for EVs at this price point. Sharing a platform with a Porsche SUV also shows VW Group pushing cost discipline even at the very top of its brand pyramid.

Counterpoints: naming and teasing a car eight-plus months ahead of its reveal, while the combustion Bentayga keeps selling alongside it, reads more like testing market appetite than a hard commitment to going electric. And building on the same bones as a mainstream-luxury Porsche SUV risks diluting the hand-built exclusivity buyers pay six figures for.

Conversation starters:
1. Does keeping the combustion Bentayga alongside the Torcal show healthy hedging, or does it suggest Bentley doesn't trust its own buyers to go electric?
2. Sharing a platform with the Cayenne Electric — smart economics, or does it undercut what people are actually paying for at this price point?
3. Does a name built around twisting rock formations and torque land with luxury buyers, or is EV naming becoming its own marketing minefield?
