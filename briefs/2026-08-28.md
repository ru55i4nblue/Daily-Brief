	**Selected: What went wrong at Williams?**

	Williams spent two seasons telling anyone who'd listen that everything was riding on the 2026 car, and the bet hasn't paid off. The FW48 arrived overweight and off the pace; the team hasn't scored since Monaco, and neither Carlos Sainz nor Alex Albon has finished higher than 15th across the last four races. For a team that treated F1's new regulations as its great reset, this is close to the nightmare scenario — and Sainz spent much of the season sounding like a driver reconsidering his options.

	Against that backdrop, Wednesday's news landed as a surprise: Sainz signed a multi-year extension reportedly worth up to €30 million (about \$35 million) a season, with exit clauses attached. He's being paid at title-contender rates to wait for the project to come good, with a door left open if it doesn't. The alternatives were thin anyway — Mercedes and McLaren are settled, Audi links never hardened, and Cadillac's debut season has been bruising, with Sergio Pérez retiring from four of the last seven races.

	It matters beyond one driver's contract: it's a signal that even a struggling constructor with title history can still command elite wages in a market with almost no open seats, and it leaves Williams with a well-paid driver but everything still to prove on track. The wider market now waits on bigger dominoes — Max Verstappen's reported performance clauses and Fernando Alonso's future remain open questions.

	**Counterpoints:** a multi-year deal at this stage locks in stability and driver development continuity that a struggling team badly needs, and Sainz's exit clauses mean the contract is less a bet on Williams than a hedge that costs him nothing if the project doesn't improve.

	**Conversation starters:**
	- Does paying near-title-contender wages to a team that hasn't scored since Monaco reflect the driver market's thinness more than Williams' prospects?
	- Do exit clauses undercut the "commitment" signal a multi-year extension is meant to send?
	- If Verstappen's or Alonso's situation resolves badly for them, does Williams become a more credible landing spot — or does Sainz's deal now block that seat?
