**Selected: Frieze Abu Dhabi and the great art land grab**

Frieze has named the 84 galleries — Gagosian, Pace, Perrotin and Victoria Miro among them — for its first Abu Dhabi fair, running 19–22 November at Manarat Al Saadiyat. It caps two decades of Saadiyat Island cultural building: the Louvre Abu Dhabi already open, a Guggenheim and the Zayed National Museum on the way, and a homegrown 17-year-old fair now folded into the Frieze brand. Art Basel opened its own Doha edition this February, meaning the world's two biggest fair franchises now sit 500km apart on the Gulf.

Why it matters: this is the clearest sign yet that Gulf capital has moved from simply buying art to building the market's institutional infrastructure — fairs, museums, galleries — shifting genuine gravitational pull away from London, New York and Basel toward Abu Dhabi and Doha, and intensifying a cultural rivalry that runs parallel to the region's economic one.

Counterpoints: state-funded scale doesn't guarantee depth — critics could call this a landgrab built on marquee international names and tourism infrastructure (the same newsletter covers a new Ritz-Carlton Reserve and Dakar-edition Land Rover) rather than an organic local collector base or artist community. Two major fairs 500km apart may also dilute attention rather than build parallel scenes, risking a zero-sum contest between Abu Dhabi and Doha instead of two thriving hubs.

Conversation starters:
- Is Gulf investment in art fairs building lasting cultural infrastructure, or renting prestige the way earlier expansion cities did?
- Can Abu Dhabi and Doha coexist as separate Gulf art hubs, or will one squeeze out the other?
- What does it mean for London, New York and Paris when both major fair franchises now have a permanent Gulf outpost?
