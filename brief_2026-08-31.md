**Selected: Istanbul is now Europe's busiest airport**

Istanbul overtook Heathrow as Europe's busiest airport in July 2026, logging 8.15 million passengers against Heathrow's 7.86 million. Heathrow had held the title almost continuously since 1996. Part of the shift is circumstantial — Heathrow's Middle East traffic dropped 20.2% amid the Iran-US conflict — but the underlying trend is structural. Istanbul was purpose-built as a mega-hub linking Europe, Asia, and Africa, feeding Turkish Airlines' network, which now serves more countries than any other carrier. A fourth runway is slated to open later this year, extending Istanbul's capacity advantage.

Heathrow, by contrast, has debated a third runway for decades; the most optimistic delivery timeline is 2035. That gap in political will and planning speed — not just one geopolitical shock — is why Istanbul's lead looks durable rather than temporary.

Why it matters: airport capacity increasingly shapes airline network strategy and national aviation competitiveness. A hub that can expand physically compounds its advantage over one that cannot, regardless of short-term traffic swings.

Counterpoints: Heathrow's dip is partly attributable to a specific, potentially reversible geopolitical event, and its premium long-haul, business-traffic base remains structurally different from Istanbul's transfer-heavy model — raw passenger counts don't fully capture route quality or yield. Istanbul's growth also depends on continued political and economic stability in Turkey, which is not guaranteed.

Conversation starters:
- Should "busiest airport" be measured by passenger volume, or does that metric obscure more important differences in traffic quality?
- Is the UK's decades-long inability to expand Heathrow a uniquely British planning failure, or symptomatic of infrastructure gridlock across mature Western economies?
- If Middle East tensions ease, does Heathrow reclaim the title, or has Istanbul's structural head start already made the shift permanent?
